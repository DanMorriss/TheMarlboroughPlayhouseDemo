A note for visitors

Welcome to Our Home

Dan & Selina  ·  Marlborough

We bought this house in 2019, when it was just Dan, Selina, and our dog Auggie. Since then, our little family has grown to include Bilby (dog number two) and our daughter Molly.


We've loved every moment here, but with Selina's parents wanting to visit more from Ireland and hopes of expanding our family further, we're looking for a little more space — which brings us to today.

The bakery, circa 1880–1890 — brought by a visitor from New Zealand whose great-grandfather worked here

A Little History

Built between 1870 and 1890, this house was originally a bakery. The original fireplace now houses a log burner, but you can still see all the levels from the old oven built into the brickwork. There's also a local story that some of the beams were salvaged from ships — and whether or not that's true, it's hard not to look at the carpenter's marks still visible on the kitchen beams and wonder where they've been. A few years ago, a visitor from New Zealand whose great-grandfather worked in the bakery came to have a look around; he brought along the photograph above.

The Location

One of the things we'll miss most is walking into town — our record is three trips to Waitrose in a single morning after forgetting ingredients for the Sunday roast. We've also been spoilt with the walks on our doorstep. Stonebridge Nature Reserve, the common and the forest are all just a few minutes away — two very happy dogs, no need to drive anywhere for a proper stretch of the legs.

The Garden

In summer we practically live outside. The garden is full of visiting birds, with a resident pigeon that nests in the conifer each year. During lockdown we added a garden office with electricity and internet — a real asset. A note on planning: there has historically been permission to extend out to the end of the stone patio. It has since lapsed (we never felt we needed it), but it's something a future owner could explore.

Why We'll Miss It

There's a character to this house you can't manufacture — the uneven floors, the thick walls, the way it stays cool in the summer. Our favourite memories are Christmas dinners: somehow squeezing 18 people around the kitchen table, everyone talking over each other, the log burner going, nobody wanting to leave. That's the magic of this place, and we hope whoever moves in next gets to feel it too.
